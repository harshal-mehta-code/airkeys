What you're hearing

Real in this prototype

  • The piano. Synthesised per note — inharmonic partial stack, per-partial decay, hammer transient, procedurally generated convolution reverb. The shipping app swaps this for multi-sampled acoustic piano.
  • The composition engine. Key and mode, a per-genre progression graph, nearest-voicing voice leading, contour-driven melody, chord-tone vs. passing-tone selection, partial quantisation and swing.
  • Genre behaviour. Switching genre changes scale, harmony, voicings, groove, tempo and colour grade. Listen to Jazz Club against Ambient.
  • The Assist slider. Drag it — quantisation, flourish density and accompaniment all move with it.

Simulated / stubbed

  • Hand tracking. No camera. The hands are procedural and either self-play or follow your pointer.
  • The camera feed. Stood in by a graded, grained field. Try Silhouette to see the camera-off treatment.
  • Predictive onset. The real system fires each note ~45 ms before your finger lands. Here, clicks are immediate.
  • Telemetry. Latency and tracking readouts are plausible fictions showing where the real numbers surface.

Where I'd like your feedback

Stage

The camera view is treated as the stage, with chrome floating over it. Right call, or should the visualiser dominate and the camera shrink to a corner?

Controls

One visible Assist slider is the only performance control. Do you want key, tempo or density surfaced too — or is one dial the point?

Genre

Chips in the top bar versus a larger carousel with audio audition on hover. Chips are compact; the carousel sells the genres harder.

Visuals

Hands render as light rather than skeletons, with particle bursts on each note. Premium, or screensaver?

Ribbon

The recent-note ribbon under the stage — genuinely useful feedback, or clutter that should go?

Record

Rec sits as a primary action because sharing is the growth loop. Too prominent for a first-run screen?
